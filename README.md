## How a sunshade panel is built

A sunshade is a stack, not a single sheet. Performance comes from the combination of face fabric, reflective or heat-absorbing layer, backing and edge construction — so the stack is agreed in writing *before* sampling.

**Layer stack (typical panel build)**

1. **Face layer** — the material a buyer touches. A reflective foil face is common where the panel sits under glass; a fabric face is used where the panel folds into a pouch.
2. **Reflective / heat-absorbing layer** — the working layer of the stack. It is selected by the buyer, not chosen by the factory.
3. **Core** — a light core (for example KT board) that gives the panel stiffness so it holds its shape over the aperture instead of collapsing onto the glass.
4. **Backing / liner** — felt or similar liner that protects the vehicle interior and stops the core edges from marking trim.
5. **Edge construction** — binding tape or a stitched hem around the perimeter; the edge is the part that fails first if it is done badly.

**Assembly**

- Panels are cut on a **flatbed cutter** (gantry plus cutting head) to the agreed pattern, with seam allowance included.
- **Computerized sewing** handles the binding, the panel seams and the edge finish on controlled stitch programs, so the same seam comes out the same way on every panel in a run.
- **Punch pressing** makes the stamped frame brackets and other metal components (perimeter frame, tension pieces, clip locations).
- Multi-layer panels are **adhesive-bonded** and then **accordion-folded**, so the product folds down to a size that fits the agreed pouch.
- Packing follows a **fit check on the target vehicle**: installation, folding, pouch size, accessory count and carton labelling are agreed on the approval sample first.

**Process steps on the shop floor**

0. Pattern & material — the agreed pattern and the agreed material stack.
1. Cutting & binding — cut, curved binding, stitching, edge finish.
2. Frame & attachments — perimeter frame, tension, clip locations.
3. Fit check & packing — trial fit on the target vehicle, agreed accessories, agreed carton.

## Ordering, MOQ and order structure

- Production starts at **100 pieces per style**. Sample quantities, sample charges and any requested tests are confirmed separately.
- A mixed order across several designs is **not** one style for the 100-piece minimum — each style is counted on its own.
- The **vehicle directory on the site is not a guarantee of fit**; geometry is confirmed against a target vehicle and an approved sample.
- Compare quotations on the same basis: same material stack, same construction, same packaging, same acceptance scope. Unsupported universal UV or temperature-reduction claims are worth ignoring.

## OEM / private-label development

The OEM/ODM route runs in five steps: specify the product and the quantity per style; make the material and construction requests comparable; confirm samples; include private-label packaging; compare the full quotation and acceptance scope. Private-label work — logo, pouch, accessory kit, instruction sheet, barcode label and carton specification — is part of the same order, not an afterthought.
